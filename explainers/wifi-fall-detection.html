📶 How Wi-Fi Can Detect a Fall

No camera and nothing to wear. The home's Wi-Fi does the watching.

The idea in one line: Wi-Fi signals bounce off our bodies. When someone falls, the signals change in a special way, and an AI learns to spot that change.

1. Wi-Fi waves fill the room

The receiver gets many copies of the signal, each taking a different path. When the person moves, the green path changes, so the signal the receiver gets changes too.

2. Each movement leaves a different “signal fingerprint”

🚶 Walking

Steady, repeating ups and downs.

⚠️ Falling

A big, fast change (about 1 second), then almost no movement.

Sitting down quickly looks a bit like a fall, but the person keeps moving afterwards. Teaching the AI to tell these apart is part of the research.

3. The 4 steps of the system

1SenseThe receiver records how the Wi-Fi signal changes, many times a second.
2CleanThe software removes noise, like random flicker in the signal.
3AI decidesA trained model labels what's happening: walking, sitting, lying down, or falling.
4AlertIf it's a fall and the person doesn't move, it sends an SMS or app alert to the family.

4. Why use Wi-Fi?

  • 🔒 Private: there's no camera, so it can even be used in a bathroom, where many falls happen.
  • 🙌 Nothing to wear: elders often forget to wear or charge a smartwatch.
  • 🌙 Works in the dark and can see through some walls.
  • 💸 Cheap: it uses Wi-Fi that most homes already have.

5. What you could research (the unsolved problems)

  • New house problem: a model trained in one room often fails in another room. Can you make it work anywhere?
  • False alarms: it needs to tell a real fall apart from sitting down quickly or dropping onto the bed.
  • More people and pets: it needs to work when others are moving in the same room.

What you'd need: 2–3 ESP32 boards (about ₹500 each), a laptop, Python, and recordings of safe, acted falls onto a mattress.
