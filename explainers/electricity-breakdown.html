Which appliance eats my electricity bill?

Short answer: every appliance leaves its own "fingerprint" in the total power your meter sees. A computer program learns these fingerprints and splits the one total into parts: this much for the AC, this much for the fridge, and so on.
The research name is NILM (Non-Intrusive Load Monitoring) = finding each appliance's share without putting a sensor on each appliance.
STEP 1

The meter sees only one line

One day in a home. Tap the buttons, then move your finger or mouse over the graph.

STEP 2

Each appliance has a fingerprint

Fridge Small bumps (~150 W), on and off every ~45 min, all day and night.
Geyser Very tall (~2000 W) but short (~30 min), mostly in the morning.
AC Big (~1100 W) and long (many hours), mostly at night.
Fan, TV & lights Small (60–300 W), mostly in the evening.
STEP 3

How the program learns to split it

  1. Spot the jumps. When the total suddenly goes up or down, something switched on or off. The size of the jump tells how big that appliance is.
  2. Match the fingerprint. "+2000 W for 30 min in the morning" → geyser. "Small bumps every 45 min" → fridge.
  3. Learn from examples. Researchers recorded real homes with a sensor on every appliance plus the total (e.g. REDD from the USA, UK-DALE from the UK). An AI model studies these and learns to split a new total by itself.

Why this can be your M.Tech project

Most training data comes from US and UK homes. Indian homes are different (geysers, mixers, inverters, many ceiling fans, voltage ups and downs), and Indian datasets are very few and small (e.g. iAWE: one house in Delhi, 73 days). A model that works well for Indian homes is a real gap.
