# Topic options, set 4: ideas that solve community problems (2026-11-07)

Shravya asked for project ideas that solve problems in the community. These are new ideas that don't repeat sets 1–3. Each one fits CSE, can be built and tested in an M.Tech, and can grow into PhD research.

| # | Idea | Problem (who suffers) | What we build | Research gap (the "new" part) | PhD path |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ambulance green corridor** | Ambulances get stuck at red signals; patients lose critical minutes | A cheap device at the signal hears the siren (audio AI), finds its direction, and turns that lane green | Hearing a siren in loud Indian traffic and finding its direction on a cheap board | Smart traffic signals that learn and cooperate across a city |
| 2 | **Leftover food → hungry people** | Weddings and hotels throw away good food while shelters go hungry | An app that matches leftover food to the nearest NGO and plans the fastest pickup | Predicting how long food stays safe, plus fast matching and routing | City-wide "food rescue" optimisation |
| 3 | **Dengue early warning** | Dengue outbreaks are noticed only after people fall sick | Predict hotspot areas from rain, temperature, complaints and mosquito sounds recorded on phones | Joining weather data with phone audio to warn days earlier | Disease forecasting AI for public health |
| 4 | **Water pipe leak finder** | Leaking pipes waste water and cause uneven supply | Cheap pressure/sound sensors on pipes; AI spots and locates leaks | Finding the leak's location with very few, cheap sensors | Self-monitoring city water networks |
| 5 | **Anaemia check by phone photo** | Many women and children have anaemia but are never tested | A photo of the inner eyelid or nails estimates anaemia risk | Working across skin tones, lighting and cheap phones | Phone-based health screening for villages |
| 6 | **School dropout early warning** | Children drop out silently; teachers notice too late | Spot at-risk students early from attendance and marks so teachers can help | Fair, explainable AI that doesn't label children unfairly | Responsible AI in education |
| 7 | **Borewell success predictor** | Farmers spend lakhs drilling borewells that turn out dry | Predict the chance of water at a spot from maps, rainfall and old borewell records | Learning from sparse, messy village data | Groundwater AI for climate resilience |
| 8 | **Garbage overflow spotter** | Overflowing bins on streets; trucks follow fixed routes | Photos or CCTV detect full bins; trucks get smart daily routes | Detecting bins in messy street images + route planning | Smart-city waste systems |
| 9 | **Emergency blood donor finder** | In emergencies families post on WhatsApp and wait for hours | Rank nearby donors by who is most likely to actually come, and alert them | Predicting real donor response, not just blood group | Health logistics and recommender systems |
| 10 | **Fire early warning for markets and slums** | Fires in crowded places spread before anyone calls for help | A cheap camera detects smoke or flame early and alerts people nearby | Fewer false alarms (cooking smoke, dust, lights) on cheap hardware | Edge AI for disaster safety |

## Recommendation
**#1 Ambulance green corridor.** It saves lives in every city, is easy to show in a demo, and is real CSE research (audio AI on a cheap device). It can grow into smart traffic research at PhD level.

Her current favourite, the **Wi-Fi fall alert / home guardian**, is also a community problem (elders living alone) and is still a strong choice.
